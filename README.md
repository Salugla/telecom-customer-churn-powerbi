\# Telecom Customer Churn Analysis | SQL \& Power BI



\## Project Overview



This project focuses on analysing customer churn in the telecommunications industry using MySQL and Microsoft Power BI. The objective is to explore customer behaviour, identify patterns associated with customer attrition, and translate data into meaningful business insights that can support customer retention strategies.



The project demonstrates an end-to-end data analytics workflow, covering data cleaning, data validation, SQL querying, exploratory data analysis, DAX calculations, KPI development, and interactive dashboard reporting.



\## Business Objectives



\- Measure overall customer churn and retention rates.

\- Identify customer segments with higher churn rates.

\- Analyse the relationship between customer tenure and churn.

\- Examine churn patterns across contract types, internet services, payment methods, and gender.

\- Explore customer monthly charges and total charges.

\- Present findings through an interactive business intelligence dashboard.

\## Data Preparation \& SQL Analysis



MySQL was used to prepare and analyse the telecommunications customer dataset.



Key activities included:



\- Imported raw customer data into a staging table.

\- Conducted data quality checks to identify missing values and duplicate customer records.

\- Investigated 11 blank values in the TotalCharges field.

\- Cleaned and transformed data before loading it into the final customer table.

\- Applied appropriate data types to numerical and categorical fields.

\- Used SQL aggregation, conditional logic, Common Table Expressions (CTEs), and window functions for analytical practice.

\- Performed exploratory analysis to examine customer churn across different customer segments.

\## Key Business Findings



The analysis identified several important customer churn patterns:



1\. \*\*New customer retention:\*\* Customers with 0–12 months of tenure recorded the highest churn rate at 47.4%.



2\. \*\*Long-term customer retention:\*\* Customers with 49+ months of tenure recorded the lowest churn rate at 9.5%.



3\. \*\*Tenure and churn relationship:\*\* Churn rates declined across the four tenure groups, indicating that newer customers represented a higher-risk segment.



These findings highlight the importance of early customer engagement and targeted retention strategies.
